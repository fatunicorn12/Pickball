# Cutscenes (the opening sequence)

The six opening scenes, from MC waking in the white void to the hand-off into gameplay on the
south crest. Everything here is generated by setup menus from data: the script, the shot list,
actor prefabs, set prefabs and a camera shot table.

- **The script** (source of every line): `Docs/Story/OpeningCutscenes.md`.
- **The shot list** (approved; framing, timing and actions per shot):
  `Docs/Story/OpeningCutscenes_ShotList.md`.
- **Art docs**: `Docs/Art/CutsceneCharacters.md` (actors, clips, faces, propeller) and
  `Docs/Art/CutsceneSets.md` (sets, markers, the void look, construction lines).

## 1. Purpose and feel

The opening introduces MC, Birdy and the broken world in two blocks the player sits through:

| Block | Scenes | Length (placeholder timing) | Starts | Ends |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | S1 The white void, S2 The lost propeller | 83.2 s | `OpeningSequence` on scene start | 1.5 s blend to the gameplay camera on Play_Spawn; the propeller platforming |
| 2 | S3 "You're the one", S4 The path, S5 The Academy, S6 The world coming apart | 187.6 s | picking up the propeller on its ledge (`PropellerPickup`) | 2.0 s blend from behind MC (the black sun in frame) to the gameplay camera on the crest |

- **Look:** S1 is pure white and shadowless. At the end of S1 the world resolves: gold
  construction lines draw the shrine, then colour floods out from MC's feet into golden hour,
  which is exactly the gameplay lighting. S2–S6 stay in golden hour.
- **Cameras:** 52 Cinemachine 3 shots, written relative to the actors and markers rather than
  to world positions, so they hold when a model or a set moves. Birdy close-ups follow a strict
  rule: a tight close-up is only allowed on his four scene-6 `[pause]` lines.
- **Subtitles:** cards of at most 2 × 42 characters, with speaker colours. Hold anywhere (touch,
  Space, Enter or gamepad South) for 1 s to skip a block.
- **During a cutscene:** player input is claimed, the gameplay HUD fades out, and the Player's
  gameplay world-space indicators are hidden (§2.4). All of it comes back exactly as it was on
  hand-back.

Writes `Logs/CutsceneFramingReport.txt` |

Run order: **1, 2, 3, 4, 4b, Rebuild Timelines, 5, 6, then Validate Framing**. Reports go to
`Logs/CutsceneBuildReport.txt`, `CutsceneTimelineReport.txt`, `CutsceneCameraBuildReport.txt` and
`CutsceneFramingReport.txt`.

A clean run gives 0 build warnings, 0 framing flags and 0 framing warnings. A second run gives
identical content. The generated scene and the `.playable` files differ only in YAML local
file ids, which are new on every run, and in `CinemachineSplineDolly` SerializeReference rids.

What `CutscenePlayer` does around a block, in order:

1. **Start:** claims `InputBlock.All` on `PlayerInputGate`; snaps the HUD CanvasGroups to hidden
   (both blocks open on a hard cut, so no HUD or debug readout shows on the first frame); hides
   the gameplay indicators (`GameplayVisuals.Hide`); turns root motion on for the actor Animators
   and records their model pose; restarts the Player's locomotion controller on its idle; takes
   the camera (`CameraHandoff.Take`: disables `SandboxOrbitCamera` and enables the
   CinemachineBrain); plays.
2. **Between timelines:** starts the next timeline on the frame the director stops.
3. **Finish or skip:** evaluates the last timeline at its end, so every actor stands on its final
   mark; puts the actor Animators' root-motion flag and model pose back; clears subtitles;
   restores the indicators; hands the camera back (the orbit camera recentres behind the Player
   and re-enables on the first frame with dt above 0); releases input; fades the HUD back; raises
   `Finished`.

**The hand-back scenes (S2, S6)** end MC's last action clip 1.0 s early with a 0.6 s ease-out, so
Timeline blends MC into the locomotion controller (restarted on its idle when the block began)
while the camera holds: the last cutscene frame is the gameplay pose. Both then blend the camera
into the orbit camera (S2 1.5 s, S6 2.0 s; `CutsceneCameraHandback`), with input released at the
start of the blend. The Player's `ChibiFootIK` is off for the whole cutscene (it planted a boot
while the timeline walked MC) and comes back 1.6 s before the last timeline ends
(`CutscenePlayer.behavioursBackBeforeEnd`). After the cut its hover correction still settles the
body by about 6 cm (S2) and 14 cm (S6) over about 6 frames, during the camera blend; the root
doesn't move.

**The gameplay indicators** (`GameplayVisuals`, wired by menu 4) are every renderer and light
under the Player that isn't part of its character model's Animator hierarchy. On the chibi
Player that is 24 renderers and 1 light:

| Object (under the Player) | What it is | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| `HealChannelVfx` (Track, Progress, HaloTrack, HaloProgress, Rune, Glow, Light, Motes, Burst, BurstRing) | the Magic heal-channel ring: the white circle and pentagram rune at the feet, its glow and light | `HealChannelVisual` |
| `DashArc` | the dash's 45° protractor flash: the light-blue arc ("shards") at the feet. Re-parents to the world on first use | `DashArc` |
| `BladeTrail` | the sword's light-blue ribbon. Re-parents to the world in Awake | `BladeTrail` |
| `Rope` | the tether line | `TetherSystem` |
| `DoubleJumpPuff` | particle puff | `MovementEffects` |
| `Skateboard` (11 Tripo parts) | the board, shown while riding | `SkateboardMount` |

Their owners hide them themselves in Play mode until they're used, so they mostly showed in
edit-mode evaluation: contact sheets, the framing validator and Timeline preview. The hide uses
`Renderer.forceRenderingOff` and a light culling mask of 0, never `enabled`, so an owner that
toggles its own renderer during a cutscene keeps that change. The restore puts back exactly the
flag and mask it found. The framing validator's contact-sheet render hides them too.

### 2.6 Conventions worth knowing

- **Units.** Plan offsets are in H (MC's measured height) and HB (Birdy's head box), resolved by
  the timeline builder from the rigged actors, so the plan scales with the model. **HB is the
  visible head**: the height of the skin weighted to the head bone (Birdy 0.280 m). It used to be
  twice the head centre's distance to the model top, which counted Birdy's propeller mast (0.529 m),
  so every Birdy framing was 1.9× looser than written and the scene-6 "TCUs" were mediums.
- **Actor Animators under Timeline.** Action tracks use zero transform offsets (not scene
  offsets), clips have foot IK off, and `CutscenePlayer` turns root motion on while it plays. With
  scene offsets and root motion off, Play mode moved MC's model up by the baked clips' root height
  (0.64 m) and he floated through scenes 2–6.
- **Birdy's hover wings are relaxed.** `BirdyClips.Hover` lowers the wings to Down-Up −0.45,
  slightly back, elbows soft, and `B_HoverIdle` adds a gentle flap. On Birdy's avatar, 0 is only
  about 20° below horizontal, so 0.3 read as a T-pose and drowned the gestures. Gesture clips set
  only the gesturing wing.
- **Every baked clip carries foot IK goals.** The Player's locomotion controller keeps running
  under the timeline (the hand-back eases into it), and its idle state has Foot IK on, which
  applies to the mixed pose. A muscle-only clip has no goals, so in Play both of MC's feet were
  pulled up to his hips in every cutscene shot (hidden by the cloak, except for a boot behind him
  in the walks: review round 2's "boot frozen at hip height"). `PoseClipBaker.BakeFootGoals`
  solves the goals per frame (evaluate with foot IK, move each goal by the miss, three passes),
  so foot IK is a no-op. `WalkCycleTests.EveryMcClipKeepsItsFeetUnderFootIK` checks it. Edit-mode
  evaluation (validator, contact sheets) runs without the controller, so it never showed this:
  check walks in Play.
- **Baked clips are keyframe-reduced.** `PoseClipBaker` samples every clip at 30 fps, then
  `CurveReducer` keeps only the keys that Hermite interpolation can't reproduce within the
  `ClipReduction` tolerances. It checks every frame and the quarter points between frames.
  - **Tolerances:** muscles 0.0018 (arm chain 0.0013); IK goals 1 mm and 0.1°; the body half of
    that. A curve that stays within tolerance of one value becomes a single key.
  - **Stored precision:** values are rounded to 1e-5, slopes to 1e-4.
  - **Result:** the 58 clips went from 172.6 MB to 15.0 MB of YAML. MC_Idle went from 527k
    lines to 25k.
  - **Fidelity:** every humanoid bone stays within 2 mm and 0.5° of an unreduced bake at 60 Hz;
    the worst is 1.89 mm and 0.35° (`ClipReductionTests`).
  - **Why the brief's 0.002 muscle tolerance was tightened:** at 0.002 the errors add up along the
    arm to just over 2 mm at a fingertip.
  - **Bake rate:** dropping it to 24 fps saved only 2%, so it stays at 30.
- **MC_Walk is not a pure copy of the gameplay walk.** The right leg replays the left leg's
  swing muscles half a cycle later (the Mixamo walk kicks the right heel above the knee on the
  chibi), and the left arm is lowered 0.2 and straightened 0.3 (muscle units) so the fist doesn't
  stick out behind the hip. `PoseClipSpec.SymmetricLegs`, `Reuse.Layer`.
- **Action blends.** Consecutive action clips blend for at least 0.4 s
  (`OpeningTimelineBuilder.MinActionBlend`; 0.25 s still read as a snap of the sword arm), except
  a change that lands on a cut, which snaps there (a blend after the cut showed MC turning on the
  spot at the head of S1_SH04).
- **Black sun.** Its first on-screen appearance is S6_SH02. Every shot from S1_SH07 to S5_SH03
  tilts (S2_SH01 pans) just enough to keep the sun's centre 10.5° outside the frame (13.5° in scene
  5): its rim ring is about 7.2° in radius. S6_SH01 keeps it just above the top edge.
- **Lens clearance.** `ShotRig` pushes the camera radially out of an actor's head sphere (0.6 HB),
  torso sphere (0.17 H) and three right-arm spheres (chest to hand, 0.07 H) whenever it is within
  the near clip + 8 cm of one. It is a no-op for the chibi, and it keeps a bigger swapped-in model
  from swallowing the lens. Birdy-frac caps can also push a camera back (`Cap`). Both pushes are
  continuous (see §5).
- **Caps are in HB, the check is on screen.** `Cap` measures Birdy's HB at his head centre's depth.
  On screen, with the helmet and a wide lens's near side, he reads about 1.28× that, so S2_SH05's
  0.22 cap shows him at about 0.28. Validate Framing reports the on-screen value.
- **Blocking.** The marks track writes the actor root. No library clip moves the root (a test
  checks this). MC's marks sit at the Player's CharacterController resting height (`skinWidth`,
  8 cm), so the Player doesn't pop up after a hand-off.
- **Facing.** `MC_SitUp` ends facing the actor's −Z, so S1_SH04 places MC turned 180° and
  S1_SH07 turns him slowly back north over 1.0–5.8 s. `MC_TurnAway` bakes a 180° body turn, so in S3_SH05
  and S3_SH06 his body faces the root's −Z.
- **Pure white void.** The gameplay post stack tonemaps (Neutral, from the pipeline asset's
  volume profile), which maps 1.0 to light grey. So the void surfaces write
  `ResolveConfig.voidWhite` (6.5, HDR) through `_CutsceneVoidWhite`, and the `VoidPost` volume,
  weighted 1 − blend, keeps that white from blooming or warming. At blend 1 the volume is off and
  gameplay post is untouched. This needs an HDR camera target, as the Game view has; a camera
  rendering into an LDR RenderTexture clamps first and shows grey.

## 3. How to tune

| To change | Edit | Then |
|---|---|---|
| A camera framing, lens, move or aim | `OpeningShotTable.asset` in the Inspector (persists), or `OpeningShotSeed.cs` and bump `Version` (reseeds the table and discards Inspector tuning) | 6, Validate Framing |
| Shot timing, holds, actions, blocking | `OpeningTimingPlan.cs` | Rebuild Timelines, 6, Validate Framing |
| A clip's pose | `McClips.cs` / `BirdyClips.cs` (muscle-space keys) | 1, Rebuild Timelines |
| Skip hold time, HUD fade | `CutscenePlaybackConfig.asset` | live |
| Subtitle size, wrap, colours, fades | `SubtitleStyle.asset` | live |
| Void fog, ambient | `VoidLook.asset` | live (Timeline preview) |
| Void whiteness | `ResolveConfig.voidWhite` (1 = old grey; at least ~5.3 after vignette = white) | live |
| Void bloom, white balance, vignette | `VoidPostFX.asset` | live |
| Construction lines, colour flood | `ResolveConfig.asset` | live |
| Mouth flap (voice level, attack/release, fallback nod) | `MC_MouthFlap.asset`, `Birdy_MouthFlap.asset` | live |
| Painted mouth thresholds, hysteresis, hold | `MC_MouthSwap.asset`, `Birdy_MouthSwap.asset` | live |
| Rotor speeds | `PropellerConfig.asset` | live |

After any change that moves a camera or an actor, run **Validate Framing**. Flags must be 0. It
checks that the subject is in the safe frame, Birdy's close-up ratio (frac), lens clearance from
the actors, grounding, hovering, overlap, the black sun, and both hand-off blends.

## 4. How to extend

### Add or change a line

1. Edit the scene's `text` block in `Docs/Story/OpeningCutscenes.md`. Ids are
   `OC_S<scene>_<nn>` in script order, so a line inserted mid-scene renumbers the lines after it.
   The importer reports added and removed ids, and keeps audio by id.
2. Run **3 Import Script**.
3. In `OpeningTimingPlan`, add `.Line("OC_Sx_nn")` to the shot that plays it, with its `Hold`.
   `TimingPlanTests` fails if a line is played twice or not at all.
4. Run **Setup Opening Cutscenes**, then **Validate Framing**.

### Add a shot

1. Add a `Shot(...)` to the scene in `OpeningTimingPlan`: beats (`Line`, `Hold`, `Wordless`),
   `Act`, `Place`/`Move`/`Walk`, and look keys. Ids must run in order (`S<scene>_SH<nn>`).
2. Add its rig to `OpeningShotSeed`, then bump `Version`.
3. Run **Setup Opening Cutscenes**, then **Validate Framing**. Run the cutscene tests.

### Drop in ElevenLabs audio

1. Save each line as `Assets/_Project/Audio/Cutscene/Opening/OC_S<scene>_<nn>.wav`, named by line
   id. The folder is the user's; the pipeline never writes it. Trim any lead-in silence that a
   `[pause]` tag generates: holds are timeline gaps, not baked audio.
2. Run **3 Import Script**. It binds clips into empty slots by id and lists the lines still on
   placeholder timing. A null clip is normal: that line keeps its placeholder duration,
   max(1.5 s, chars/15) + 0.3 s.
3. Run **Rebuild Timelines**, then **6 Build Cameras**. Each shot re-times as audio + the same
   holds + the same action time. Then run **Validate Framing**.
4. Re-check the scene-4 olive-trunk stations (`SetLayout.TrunkStations`): they are spaced for the
   placeholder timing (`Docs/Art/CutsceneSets.md` §6).

### Swap a model (checked with the Tripo guardian Euler as MC, review round 4)

1. Run **Setup Opening Cutscenes** so `Scenes/Cutscene/OpeningPlayable.unity` is current, and open
   it.
2. Unpack the Player prefab instance completely, in the scene only. Never edit `Chibi.prefab`.
3. Instantiate the new humanoid FBX under the Player root at the old model's local position and
   rotation. Its Animator needs a Humanoid avatar. Then delete the old model (`ChibiModel`).
4. Run **4b Rig Scene Actors**. It re-measures H and HB from the bind pose, moves the anchors and
   the mouth flap onto the new bones, removes `EyeSwap` when no painted eye variant matches the
   texture, and puts `Blade` on the hand when there is no `WeaponSocket`.
5. Run **Rebuild Timelines**, then **6 Build Cameras**. Save the scene.
6. Run **Validate Framing** as its own step. When a script ran it in the same editor call as the
   swap and the rebuilds, S6_SH12's insert sampled the new model's hand 0.8 m off and gave 9
   flags that a separate run does not reproduce.
7. Read the flags. They are framing consequences of the new size, not pipeline errors. Fix them
   by tuning the rigs (§3), never in the scene.
8. Look at it in Play mode. The swapped scene is saved, so Play uses it.
9. To revert, run **Setup Opening Cutscenes**. Step 4 rebuilds the scene with the gameplay Player.

**Result with Euler** (H 1.697 → 2.100 m, HB 0.574 → 0.748 m): 0 build errors, **0 flags**,
0 warnings. Three earlier flag sources were validator or rig assumptions about size, now fixed:
- the S6_SH12 blade insert: the actor bounds counted the Player's hidden gameplay indicators
  (the heal ring's 2.4 m glow box);
- the toe band was fixed at −4 cm and Euler's toe bones sit 4.3 cm below his root;
- S4_SH02, which used to lose his taller head off the left edge, no longer flags. It wasn't tuned for this; it passes after this round's other changes.

The clearance also covers the right arm now. In Play, all of scene 1 runs on him: lying, OTS
reach and flood (`Builds/Cutscenes/frames/r4/swap_euler.png`, from `Swap_Euler.mp4`). He has no
locomotion controller, so the hand-backs are not representative.

**A scene-only swap is temporary.** Step 4 (Build Opening Scene) regenerates the scene from the
greybox and puts the gameplay Player back. A lasting MC swap belongs in the gameplay Player
itself. For the standalone `MC_Actor`/`Birdy_Actor`, see `Docs/Art/CutsceneCharacters.md`
("Swapping a model").

### Swap a set (checked with a placeholder Void, review round 4)

1. Build the replacement as a prefab, with its root at the world origin and its content in world
   coordinates. The placeholder that worked had:
   - **geometry:** a white sphere (radius 40 m) at MC_Start, no collider, no shadows. Its unlit
     material has HDR colour 6.5 and front faces culled, so the inside renders. The
     `Pickball/Cutscene/Void Surface` material reads `_CutsceneVoidWhite` for you.
   - **`CutsceneLook`** on the root, `Configure`d with `VoidLook`, `GoldenHourLook`,
     `ResolveConfig`, the flood centre (the sphere) and the void-only renderers (the sphere), so
     they switch off at blend 1.
   - **`VoidPost`:** a child with a global `Volume` (priority 10) using `VoidPostFX`, wired with
     `CutsceneLook.ConfigurePost`.
   - **markers:** a `CutsceneMarker` for every marker id the original set provides, at the same
     poses. Copy them from the original prefab. The Void provides none.
   - **`CutsceneSet`** on the root with the original's `SetId` and those markers.
2. Point the set's entry in `ScriptableObjects/Cutscene/Playback/CutsceneSetList.asset` at the new
   prefab.
3. Run **Setup Opening Cutscenes**, then **Validate Framing**. The build report lists any marker
   id the shots use that no set provides.
4. Look at scene 1 to scene 2 in Play mode.
5. To revert, point the entry back, or delete the asset and run Setup again: menu 4 reseeds it
   from `PipelinePaths.SetIds`. The reseeded list is identical to the committed one.

**Result:** 0 build warnings, **0 flags**, 0 warnings. In Play, scene 1 plays white with MC
lying, the OTS reach, and the flood resolving into the shrine on time
(`Builds/Cutscenes/frames/r4/swap_void.png`, from `Swap_Void.mp4`). What happens when a part is
missing:
- with a bare white sphere and no `CutsceneLook`, the build gives 6 warnings ("no CutsceneLook …
  look.blend track skipped", one per timeline). Scene 1 renders in golden hour with the shrine
  visible, and the sphere never hides, so scene 2 plays inside it;
- with `CutsceneLook` but no volume, the void works and hides on time, but its HDR white blooms
  over MC.

### Reusing Birdy's rig on a replacement Birdy

Birdy's 22 humanoid-mapped bones and the unmapped ones are listed in
`Docs/Art/CutsceneCharacters.md` ("Birdy's bones"). In short, his Humanoid map is:

| Body | Bones |
|---|---|
| Hips and spine | `Hip`; `Waist`, `Spine01`, `Spine02` |
| Neck and head | `NeckTwist01`, `Head` |
| Arms (his wings) | `L/R_Clavicle`, `L/R_Upperarm`, `L/R_Forearm`, `L/R_Hand` |
| Legs | `L/R_Thigh`, `L/R_Calf`, `L/R_Foot`, `L/R_ToeBase` |

He has no jaw, eye or finger bones. Every MC and Birdy clip is a Humanoid muscle clip, so it
plays on any Humanoid with no retarget step. These parts do **not** carry over to another model:
- **`Rotor` bone.** `BirdyModelPostprocessor` adds it under `Head`. Without it, 4b sets no
  `PropellerSocket` and adds no `CutsceneRotor`: no rotor spin, and the skinned propeller isn't
  hidden while the loose prop flies.
- **The loose `Propeller` mesh.** It is extracted from `Birdy.fbx` (`tripo_part_1`, in the Rotor
  frame) by `Tools/Blender/cutscene_extract_propeller.py`.
- **`EyeSwap` faces (eye variants and the painted mouths).** All six per character are painted on
  that model's own atlas, so a replacement gets none: no `EyeSwap`, no `MouthSwap`.
- **Lip-sync.** Without painted mouths the mouth flap falls back to nodding the head bone (or a
  humanoid Jaw); it needs re-tuning per model.
- **`B_HardLanding`.** It is baked from a Mixamo clip imported with its own Mixamo avatar (Copy
  From Other Avatar can't apply). The baked copy is a muscle clip and does retarget; only
  re-baking it needs that source.

### Add a speaker

1. Add the id to `CutsceneSpeakers`, spelled as the script writes it before the colon.
2. Add a `SubtitleSpeakerStyle` (id, display name, colour) to `SubtitleStyle.asset`.
3. Give the speaker an actor:
   - a prefab with a `CutsceneActor` of that id (4b rigs any placed `CutsceneActor`) and a
     `<Name>_ActionLibrary.asset`;
   - placement in `OpeningSceneBuilder.BuildActors`;
   - a blocking track in `OpeningTimelineBuilder.BuildBlocking`, whose actor loop is MC, BIRDY,
     PROPELLER;
   - an id in `PlanActors` for the timing plan.
4. Write the lines and run **Setup Opening Cutscenes**.

## 5. Known limitations and open questions

- **Verify in the Play-mode recording, not in edit-mode stills (review round 3).** Two round-2
  fixes I had checked with edit-mode renders still failed in the recording. Both turned out to be
  verification gaps, not Play/edit differences: the same evaluation in edit mode (director
  evaluated, brain updated by hand) gives the same camera and look numbers as Play.
  - *S1_SH07's flood.* The look blend ran 4.0–6.0 s correctly in Play. But the flood radius
    (`ResolveConfig` 55 m, exponent 1) covered the visible lawn in the first ~15% of the blend
    (~0.3 s), and a smoothstep sky fade left the sky white until ~1.2 s in. A mid-shot still (3.0 s)
    is before the flood, so it could not show this. Now: exponent 1.5 to 80 m, and a cubic
    ease-out sky fade.
  - *S6_SH13's push.* The shot's Birdy frac cap (0.28) held the camera back at 3.1 m from MC
    for the whole shot (Birdy hovers 1.6 m off MC), so the keyed MCU→CU push never happened, in
    edit mode as in Play. A single mid-shot still can't show a push. The cap is gone from the shot.
  - Play really does differ from edit mode in two places: the gameplay controller runs under the
    timeline in Play (see the foot IK note in §2.6), and a key hung off MC's head follows his
    controller pose in his last second in Play, but his default pose in edit mode. So check
    motion, timing and anything after a hand-back in the recording, with frames extracted at the
    reviewer's timestamps (`Builds/Cutscenes/frames/r3/`).
- **The Birdy frac cap and the lens clearance must stay continuous.** The cap used to push the
  lens a full step the moment Birdy crossed the camera plane, and the clearance used to push back
  along the view axis, which flips through a sphere when the lens faces away from it. Either one
  makes a one-frame pop: S2_SH10 popped 1.4 m twice (review round 3), and S3_SH07 jumped too. The
  cap now fades in over the first quarter of the needed depth, and the clearance pushes radially.
